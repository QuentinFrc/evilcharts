## Usage

Compose a `<Tolerance />` inside an area or line chart to draw what the series is expected to hold
to: a faint band over the accepted region, its bounds, and — unless `highlightBreaches` is false —
the runs that left it repainted.

One part covers a ceiling, a floor and a corridor, because they are one shape with a bound at
infinity. `max` alone accepts everything below it, `min` alone everything above it, both together
the interval between. There is no side to pass — which bound you give is the side — and no variant
to pick, so the part never decides whether a region is good or bad. You declare what is accepted,
the band paints it, the overlay marks what leaves it. What that means is yours, and lives in the
config keys you hand it.

```tsx
import { EChartsAreaChart } from "@/components/evilcharts/charts/echarts-area-chart";

<EChartsAreaChart data={data} config={chartConfig} xDataKey="hour">
  <EChartsAreaChart.Tolerance min={2} max={8} dataKey="safe" breachKey="excursion" label="2 – 8 °C" />
  <EChartsAreaChart.Area dataKey="degrees" variant="gradient" />
</EChartsAreaChart>;
```

## One bound

Give only `max` and everything below it is accepted — a ceiling. Give only `min` and everything
above it is — a floor. The missing bound is infinity: the band runs to the edge of the plot, no line
is drawn where there is nothing to draw, and the overlay marks what crossed the bound you did give.

## Breaches

By default the runs outside the band are repainted, with a dot on each point that left it. Turn it
off and the band and its bounds carry the reading on their own.

## Color

`dataKey` paints the band and its bounds, `breachKey` the runs that left. Omit them and the band
takes the muted foreground while the departures take the plain one — quiet band, loud exceptions,
which is the reading either way.

## Intent

Nothing in the part says that inside the band is the good place to be. `dataKey` colors the region
you declared acceptable and `breachKey` the runs that left it; what those two keys mean is yours.
Swap them and the same shape carries the opposite instruction.

A cash position is overdrawn below zero. There the band is the region to climb out of, and the
runs the overlay picks out are the weeks that managed it — a band marking what you want to leave,
drawn by exactly the same part as one marking what you want to hold.

## Several series

The band belongs to the chart, the departures to each series. One norm is laid over the plot once,
and the overlay is built per series, so two probes in the same corridor each carry their own runs —
one leaving it from above, the other from below — in the same breach color.

## Several subjects

A chart can carry more than one norm, as long as they judge different things. `for` names the
series a norm applies to; without it a norm judges every series, which is what a lone
`<Tolerance />` does today. Two norms over the same series collapse to the first, with a warning in
development — a series is read against one norm.

Once a chart carries more than one, the bands lose their fill. Two translucent regions over one
axis make a third tint where they meet that belongs to neither, and a reader cannot tell which
region they are in; the bounds carry the reading on their own, dashed so a lone line does not read
as a series. Each norm keeps its own `breachKey`, so the runs that left say which norm they left.

## API Reference

<ApiTable>
  <ApiRow name="min" type="number" default="-Infinity">
    Lower bound of what is accepted, in y-axis units. Omitted, the region is unbounded below.
  </ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="max" type="number" default="Infinity">
    Upper bound. Omitted, the region is unbounded above. At least one bound is required; with
    neither, nothing is drawn and development warns. Bounds given the wrong way round describe the
    same region and are honored as such.
  </ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="target" type="number">
    The value aimed at, drawn as a dashed line. Not necessarily the middle of the region.
  </ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="dataKey" type="string">
    Config key whose color paints the band and its bounds. Without one the band paints in the muted
    foreground color. On the Recharts roots a config key must also be a key of the row type, so a
    color-only entry is declared on that type.
  </ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="breachKey" type="string">
    Config key whose color paints the runs that left. Without one they paint in the foreground
    color. It never falls back to `dataKey`: a band that reads as loudly as the departures from it
    reads as nothing at all.
  </ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="label" type="string">
    Text set on the outer bound — the upper one when there is one, the lower one on a floor.
  </ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="for" type="string" default="—">
    The series this norm judges, by `dataKey`. Omitted, it judges every series on the chart. Two
    norms over the same series collapse to the first, with a warning in development; norms over
    different series are all drawn, and the bands lose their fill so the bounds can be told apart.
  </ApiRow>
  <ApiRow name="highlightBreaches" type="boolean" default="true">
    Repaints the series runs outside the band, with a dot on each point that left it. The band and
    its bounds still read without it.
  </ApiRow>
</ApiTable>

## Notes

A chart is read against one norm. Compose several `<Tolerance />` on one chart and only the first is
drawn, identically on both providers, with a warning in development — several bands is not one norm:
the runs outside each would repaint the series one over the other, and their colors would come from
unrelated config entries rather than a severity ramp. An ordered ladder of levels is a different
part.

A bound outside the data range pulls the axis out to reach it — a bound you cannot see is a bound
you cannot judge against. With both bounds the axis is also padded until the band takes up no more
than 60% of it: a series that holds its range would otherwise set an extent equal to the band, and a
band that fills the plot reads as the plot. A one-sided band has no width to take a share of, so it
only gets the usual slack past its bound, and a series that roams further keeps its own scale either
way.

Where the part does set an axis edge, it rounds that edge outward to a round step of the span's own
magnitude. The edge it computes is an arithmetic result, not a number anyone wrote, and a renderer
prints it verbatim; rounding costs nothing — the edge only ever moves further out — and gives back
legible ticks.

On a stacked area chart the overlay is skipped: there a series' own value is not its plotted height,
so a per-series departure would point at the wrong place. The band and its bounds still read.

The band carries no border. Where it is bounded its own fill edge already delimits it; a border
would additionally stroke the left and right edges of the plot, which say nothing, and on an open
side it would trace the frame itself. The bound lines are what make those edges crisp at a tenth of
an opacity, and what carry the label.

A departure sits right beside the band it left, so the two colors have to stay apart. The usual
green-against-red pairing does not: under deuteranopia it collapses to roughly 6 ΔE, and this repo's
own dark `--chart-2` and `--destructive` sit at an identical lightness. So a departure also carries
dots — color is never its only channel.
